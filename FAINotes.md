## 1. What is AI? State Applications of AI.

**Artificial Intelligence (AI)** refers to the simulation of human intelligence in machines that are designed to think, learn, and make decisions. AI encompasses various technologies and methodologies to replicate human-like cognition, such as reasoning, problem-solving, and understanding language. AI can be broadly divided into three categories based on its capabilities: Artificial Narrow Intelligence (ANI), Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), and Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI).

**Applications of AI:**
- **Healthcare:** AI-powered tools analyze medical images, predict diseases, and suggest personalized treatment plans. AI also assists in drug discovery and robotic surgery.
- **Education:** AI tailors educational content to individual students' needs, automates grading, and provides virtual tutoring.
- **Business and Finance:** Chatbots for customer service, fraud detection systems, market analysis, and process automation.
- **Transportation:** Self-driving cars, traffic management, and route optimization for logistics.
- **Entertainment:** Content recommendations on platforms like Netflix, AI in game development, and film production.
- **Retail:** Personalized shopping recommendations, inventory management, and AI-powered chatbots.
- **Agriculture:** Precision farming, automated machinery, and yield prediction.
- **Environment:** Climate modeling, wildlife conservation, and optimization of renewable energy.
- **Security and Defense:** Surveillance systems, cybersecurity, and military applications.
- **Social Media:** Content moderation, sentiment analysis, and targeted advertising.
- **Science and Research:** Data analysis in physics, biology, astronomy, and space exploration.

## 2. Discuss types of AI in detail.

**Types of AI:**

1. **Artificial Narrow Intelligence (ANI):**
   - **Definition:** ANI, also known as Weak AI, refers to AI systems designed to perform specific tasks within a pre-defined range. These systems lack the ability to generalize beyond their programming.
   - **Characteristics:**
     - Specializes in one domain or task (e.g., image recognition, language translation).
     - Operates based on pre-programmed rules or trained data.
     - Lacks self-awareness, consciousness, and general problem-solving abilities.
   - **Examples:** Virtual assistants (Siri, Alexa), recommendation systems (Netflix, Spotify), image recognition software, and autonomous vehicles (current stage).

2. **Artificial General Intelligence (AGI):**
   - **Definition:** AGI, or Strong AI, refers to AI systems with the ability to understand, learn, and perform any intellectual task that a human can do. AGI systems can generalize knowledge and apply it across various domains.
   - **Characteristics:**
     - Possesses cognitive abilities comparable to humans.
     - Capable of reasoning, problem-solving, and learning from experiences.
     - Displays self-awareness and independent decision-making.
   - **State of Development:** AGI is currently theoretical and has not been achieved. Research is ongoing in machine learning, deep learning, and neuroscience to create AGI.

3. **Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI):**
   - **Definition:** ASI refers to AI systems that surpass human intelligence in all aspects, including creativity, problem-solving, and decision-making.
   - **Characteristics:**
     - Superior intellectual abilities compared to humans.
     - Can innovate and create solutions beyond human capabilities.
     - Operates with complete autonomy and self-awareness.
   - **State of Development:** ASI remains speculative and is the subject of philosophical and ethical debates. No existing technology suggests ASI is achievable in the near future.
   - **Potential Impact:**
     - **Positive:** Solving global problems, advancing science and technology.
     - **Risks:** Loss of human control, ethical dilemmas, and existential risks.

## 3. Write a note on Intelligent Agent.

**Intelligent Agent (IA):**
An Intelligent Agent is a system that perceives its environment through sensors and acts upon it using actuators to achieve specific goals autonomously. These agents use AI techniques like machine learning, reasoning, and decision-making to optimize their actions.

**Characteristics of Intelligent Agents:**
1. **Autonomy:** Operates without human intervention.
2. **Perception:** Uses sensors to gather data from the environment.
3. **Action:** Uses actuators to influence the environment.
4. **Goal-Oriented Behavior:** Acts to achieve specific objectives.
5. **Adaptability:** Can learn and improve performance over time.
6. **Persistence:** Operates continuously over time.
7. **Interactivity:** May communicate with humans or other agents.
8. **Rationality:** Makes decisions that maximize expected performance.

**Types of AI Agents:**
1. **Simple Reflex Agents:** Work on condition-action rules (if-then rules). Example: A thermostat.
2. **Model-Based Reflex Agents:** Maintain an internal model of the world to handle partially observable environments. Example: A self-driving car.
3. **Goal-Based Agents:** Make decisions by considering future consequences of actions. Example: A chess-playing AI.
4. **Utility-Based Agents:** Optimize decisions based on a utility function. Example: An investment AI.
5. **Learning Agents:** Improve performance over time using machine learning. Example: Netflix recommendation system.

**Perception-Action Cycle:**
The perception-action cycle is a continuous loop where an AI system perceives its environment, processes the information, and takes actions based on that perception. This cycle allows the AI to interact dynamically with its surroundings and adapt its behavior in real-time to achieve goals.

## 5. What is Knowledge representation? Explain Types of Knowledge.

**Knowledge Representation (KR):**
Knowledge representation in AI refers to encoding information about the world into formats that AI systems can utilize to solve complex tasks. It enables machines to reason, learn, and make decisions by structuring data in a way that mirrors human understanding.

**Types of Knowledge:**
1. **Declarative Knowledge (Descriptive Knowledge):**
   - Consists of facts and information about the world. Represents "what" is known.
   - Example: "Paris is the capital of France."

2. **Procedural Knowledge:**
   - Defines the steps or methods required to perform specific tasks. Represents "how" to accomplish something.
   - Example: "How to solve a quadratic equation."

3. **Inheritance Knowledge:**
   - Refers to the ability to pass down properties, attributes, and behaviors from one entity to another within a hierarchical structure.
   - Example: "A dog is a mammal, and mammals are animals."

4. **Relational Knowledge:**
   - Understanding how different entities, concepts, or objects are connected.
   - Example: "A customer places an order."

5. **Inferential Knowledge:**
   - The ability to derive new knowledge based on existing facts, rules, and logical reasoning.
   - Example: "If it is raining, then the ground is wet."

6. **Heuristic Knowledge (Experience-Based Knowledge):**
   - Derived from experience, intuition, and trial-and-error methods.
   - Example: "If lost in a city, follow the biggest road—it likely leads to the city center."

7. **Tacit Knowledge:**
   - Knowledge that is difficult to articulate or transfer, rooted in personal experience and intuition.
   - Example: A doctor's intuition about a diagnosis.

8. **Explicit Knowledge:**
   - Knowledge that can be easily articulated, documented, and shared.
   - Example: Textbooks, manuals, mathematical formulas.

9. **Research Knowledge:**
   - Knowledge acquired through systematic investigation and experimentation.
   - Example: Findings from clinical trials in medical research.

## 6. What are the various approaches towards knowledge representation?

**Approaches to Knowledge Representation:**

1. **Logical Representation:**
   - Uses formal logic to represent knowledge and enable reasoning.
   - **Types:**
     - **Propositional Logic:** Uses simple true/false statements. Example: "If it is raining, then the ground is wet."
     - **First-Order Logic (FOL):** Allows use of objects, properties, and relations. Example: "All humans are mortal. Socrates is human. Therefore, Socrates is mortal."
     - **Fuzzy Logic:** Deals with degrees of truth rather than binary values.
   - **Advantages:** Enables logical reasoning, basis for programming languages.
   - **Disadvantages:** Can be restrictive and inefficient for complex tasks.

2. **Semantic Network Representation:**
   - Represents knowledge as graphical networks with nodes (objects) and arcs (relationships).
   - Example: "Jerry is a cat. Jerry is owned by Priya."
   - **Advantages:** Natural representation, easy to understand.
   - **Disadvantages:** Computationally intensive, lacks standard definitions for links.

3. **Frame Representation:**
   - Organizes knowledge into structured frames with slots (attributes) and values.
   - Example: A "Car" frame with slots like Make, Model, Color, Year.
   - **Advantages:** Flexible, easy to add new attributes.
   - **Disadvantages:** Inference mechanisms can be complex.

4. **Production Rules:**
   - Represents knowledge as if-then rules.
   - Example: "IF a patient has a fever AND cough, THEN consider flu."
   - **Advantages:** Modular, easy to modify.
   - **Disadvantages:** Does not store results for future use, can be inefficient.

## 7. Define NLP, Applications of NLP & Challenges in NLP.

**Natural Language Processing (NLP):**
NLP is a machine learning technology that gives computers the ability to interpret, manipulate, and comprehend human language. It focuses on the interaction between computers and human language, enabling machines to understand, interpret, and generate human language meaningfully.

**Applications of NLP:**
1. **Chatbots & Virtual Assistants:** Siri, Alexa, Google Assistant.
2. **Machine Translation:** Google Translate, DeepL.
3. **Sentiment Analysis:** Analyzing social media comments, product reviews.
4. **Speech Recognition:** Converting spoken words into text (e.g., Google Voice Typing).
5. **Text Summarization:** Generating concise summaries of long documents.
6. **Search Engines & Information Retrieval:** Improving search accuracy by understanding user intent.
7. **Content Moderation:** Detecting and removing harmful content on social media.
8. **Healthcare:** Extracting insights from medical records, assisting in diagnoses.

**Challenges in NLP:**
1. **Ambiguity:** Words can have multiple meanings depending on context.
   - Example: "I saw a man with a telescope." (Who has the telescope?)
2. **Language Variations:** Different regions and social groups use language differently.
3. **Context Dependence:** NLP models struggle with sarcasm, idioms, and cultural references.
   - Example: "I just love waiting in long lines!" (Sarcastic)
4. **Polysemy and Homonyms:** Words with multiple meanings.
   - Example: "The bank is closed." (Financial bank or riverbank?)
5. **Computational Complexity and Cost:** Training large NLP models requires massive resources.
6. **Ethical and Privacy Concerns:** Misuse of AI-generated text for misinformation or deepfakes.

Pattern recognition in AI refers to the ability of machines to recognize patterns and regularities in data. It enables computers to analyze input data (images, speech, text) and identify recurring structures, trends, or similarities.

**Relevance in Machine Learning:**
- **Foundation of ML:** Pattern recognition is the core of ML algorithms, enabling them to learn from data and make predictions.
- **Feature Extraction:** ML models rely on pattern recognition to identify relevant features from raw data.
- **Applications:**
  - **Computer Vision:** Face recognition, object detection.
  - **Speech and Language Processing:** Voice recognition, sentiment analysis.
  - **Healthcare:** Disease diagnosis from medical scans.
  - **Finance:** Fraud detection, stock market prediction.
  - **Cybersecurity:** Identifying cyber threats based on past patterns.

**Steps in Pattern Recognition:**
1. Data Acquisition
2. Preprocessing
3. Feature Extraction
4. Pattern Classification
5. Post-processing & Decision Making

## 9. Discuss Components of Expert Systems and their Functions.

**Expert System:** An expert system is a computer-based system that mimics human expert decision-making in a specific domain. It uses knowledge and inference rules to analyze data and provide recommendations or solutions.

**Components of Expert Systems:**
1. **Knowledge Base (KB):**
   - Stores facts, rules, and heuristics related to the domain.
   - **Types of Knowledge:**
     - Factual Knowledge: Objective information (e.g., symptoms of diseases).
     - Heuristic Knowledge: Rules of thumb (e.g., "If a patient has a fever and cough, consider flu").
   - **Function:** Provides the system with the necessary information to make decisions.

2. **Inference Engine:**
   - The "brain" of the expert system that applies reasoning to the knowledge base.
   - **Reasoning Approaches:**
     - Forward Chaining: Starts with known facts and applies rules to reach a conclusion.
     - Backward Chaining: Starts with a hypothesis and works backward to verify it.
   - **Function:** Processes the knowledge to derive conclusions or recommendations.

3. **User Interface (UI):**
   - Allows users to interact with the system through questions, inputs, and result displays.
   - **Function:** Facilitates communication between the user and the system.

4. **Explanation Facility:**
   - Explains the reasoning behind a decision, increasing trust and interpretability.
   - **Function:** Helps users understand how the system arrived at a conclusion.

5. **Knowledge Acquisition Module:**
   - Updates the knowledge base with new facts, rules, and expert insights.
   - **Function:** Ensures the system remains up-to-date with the latest knowledge.

**Examples of Expert Systems:**
1. **MYCIN:** Diagnoses bacterial infections and recommends antibiotics.
2. **DENDRAL:** Identifies molecular structures of chemical compounds.
3. **PROSPECTOR:** Predicts mineral deposits in geology.
4. **XCON:** Configures computer systems based on customer specifications.

## 11. Write a note on supervised learning.

**Supervised Learning:**
Supervised learning is a type of machine learning where the model is trained on labeled data, meaning the input data is paired with the correct output. The goal is for the model to learn a mapping from inputs to outputs so that it can predict the output for new, unseen data.

**Key Characteristics:**
1. **Labeled Data:** The training dataset includes input-output pairs (e.g., images with labels, emails marked as spam or not spam).
2. **Objective:** Learn a function that maps inputs to outputs based on the training data.
3. **Training Process:** The model adjusts its parameters to minimize the difference between its predictions and the actual labels.
4. **Evaluation:** The model's performance is measured on a separate test dataset to ensure generalization.

**Types of Supervised Learning:**
1. **Classification:**
   - Predicts discrete class labels.
   - Examples:
     - Binary Classification: Spam detection (spam or not spam).
     - Multi-Class Classification: Handwritten digit recognition (0-9).
2. **Regression:**
   - Predicts continuous numerical values.
   - Examples:
     - Predicting house prices based on features like size and location.
     - Forecasting stock prices.

**Algorithms in Supervised Learning:**
1. **Linear Regression:** Predicts continuous values by fitting a linear relationship between inputs and outputs.
2. **Logistic Regression:** Used for binary classification by estimating probabilities.
3. **Decision Trees:** Splits data into branches to make predictions based on feature values.
4. **Support Vector Machines (SVM):** Finds the optimal boundary between classes for classification.
5. **Neural Networks:** Models complex relationships using layers of interconnected neurons.

**Applications:**
1. **Healthcare:** Disease diagnosis based on patient data.
2. **Finance:** Credit scoring, fraud detection.
3. **Marketing:** Customer segmentation, churn prediction.
4. **Image Recognition:** Object detection, facial recognition.
5. **Natural Language Processing:** Sentiment analysis, text classification.

**Advantages:**
- Clear objectives with labeled data.
- Well-understood and widely used.
- Can achieve high accuracy with sufficient data.

**Disadvantages:**
- Requires large amounts of labeled data, which can be expensive to obtain.
- May overfit if the model is too complex or the dataset is small.
- Limited to problems where labeled data is available.

## 12. Discuss various Linear Regression Models.

**Linear Regression Models:**
Linear regression is a statistical method used to model the relationship between a dependent variable and one or more independent variables by fitting a linear equation to the observed data.

**Types of Linear Regression Models:**

1. **Simple Linear Regression:**
   - Models the relationship between one independent variable (X) and one dependent variable (Y).
   - Equation: \( Y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 X + \epsilon \)
     - \( \beta_0 \): Intercept.
     - \( \beta_1 \): Slope.
     - \( \epsilon \): Error term.
   - Example: Predicting a student's test score (Y) based on hours studied (X).

2. **Multiple Linear Regression:**
   - Extends simple linear regression to multiple independent variables.
   - Equation: \( Y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 X_1 + \beta_2 X_2 + ... + \beta_n X_n + \epsilon \)
   - Example: Predicting house prices (Y) based on size (X1), location (X2), and age (X3).

3. **Polynomial Regression:**
   - Models non-linear relationships by adding polynomial terms.
   - Equation: \( Y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 X + \beta_2 X^2 + ... + \beta_n X^n + \epsilon \)
   - Example: Modeling the growth rate of plants over time where growth is not linear.

4. **Ridge Regression (L2 Regularization):**
   - Addresses multicollinearity (high correlation among independent variables) by adding a penalty term to the loss function.
   - Loss Function: \( \text{MSE} + \lambda \sum_{i=1}^n \beta_i^2 \)
     - \( \lambda \): Regularization parameter.
   - Example: Predicting stock prices with many correlated features.

5. **Lasso Regression (L1 Regularization):**
   - Similar to ridge regression but uses absolute values for the penalty term, which can shrink some coefficients to zero (feature selection).
   - Loss Function: \( \text{MSE} + \lambda \sum_{i=1}^n |\beta_i| \)
   - Example: Selecting important features in a high-dimensional dataset.

6. **Elastic Net Regression:**
   - Combines L1 and L2 regularization to handle multicollinearity and perform feature selection.
   - Loss Function: \( \text{MSE} + \lambda_1 \sum_{i=1}^n |\beta_i| + \lambda_2 \sum_{i=1}^n \beta_i^2 \)
   - Example: Datasets with many features where some are correlated.

**Assumptions of Linear Regression:**
1. Linearity: The relationship between variables is linear.
2. Independence: Observations are independent of each other.
3. Homoscedasticity: Constant variance of errors.
4. Normality: Errors are normally distributed.
5. No Multicollinearity: Independent variables are not highly correlated.

**Applications:**
1. Economics: Predicting GDP growth based on factors like inflation and unemployment.
2. Healthcare: Estimating patient recovery time based on treatment and demographics.
3. Marketing: Forecasting sales based on advertising spend.
4. Engineering: Predicting material strength based on composition and processing.

## 13. List Applications of SVM.

**Support Vector Machines (SVM):**
SVM is a supervised learning algorithm used for classification and regression tasks. It works by finding the optimal hyperplane that separates data points of different classes with the maximum margin.

**Applications of SVM:**
1. **Image Classification:**
   - Handwritten digit recognition (e.g., postal mail sorting).
   - Object detection in images.
2. **Text and Hypertext Categorization:**
   - Spam detection in emails.
   - Sentiment analysis of social media posts.
3. **Bioinformatics:**
   - Protein classification.
   - Cancer diagnosis based on gene expression data.
4. **Face Detection:**
   - Identifying faces in images or videos.
5. **Handwriting Recognition:**
   - Converting handwritten text into digital format.
6. **Medical Diagnosis:**
   - Classifying medical images (e.g., tumors in MRI scans).
   - Predicting disease outcomes based on patient data.
7. **Financial Forecasting:**
   - Stock market trend prediction.
   - Credit scoring for loan approvals.
8. **Geospatial Analysis:**
   - Land cover classification in satellite imagery.
9. **Speech Recognition:**
   - Converting spoken language into text.
10. **Industrial Applications:**
    - Fault detection in manufacturing processes.
    - Quality control in production lines.

**Advantages of SVM:**
- Effective in high-dimensional spaces.
- Works well with clear margin of separation.
- Robust to overfitting, especially in high-dimensional space.
- Versatile (can use different kernel functions for non-linear classification).

**Disadvantages of SVM:**
- Not suitable for large datasets due to high computational cost.
- Requires careful tuning of hyperparameters (e.g., kernel, regularization).
- Poor performance on noisy or overlapping datasets.

## 14. Differentiate between Classification and Regression.

**Classification vs. Regression:**

| Feature          | Classification                                                                 | Regression                                                                 |
|------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Objective**    | Predicts discrete class labels (categories).                                   | Predicts continuous numerical values.                                      |
| **Output Type**  | Categorical (e.g., spam/not spam, dog/cat/bird).                               | Numerical (e.g., house price, temperature).                                |
| **Examples**     | Email spam detection, image recognition, disease diagnosis.                    | House price prediction, stock market forecasting, temperature prediction.  |
| **Algorithms**   | Logistic Regression, Decision Trees, SVM, Naive Bayes, Neural Networks.        | Linear Regression, Polynomial Regression, Ridge/Lasso Regression, Neural Networks. |
| **Evaluation Metrics** | Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score, Confusion Matrix.                  | Mean Squared Error (MSE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), R-squared.       |
| **Nature of Problem** | Deals with assigning inputs to specific categories.                        | Deals with predicting a quantity or value.                                 |
| **Decision Boundary** | Often involves finding boundaries between classes.                           | Involves fitting a line or curve to the data.                              |

**Example Scenarios:**
- **Classification:** Determining if an email is spam or not spam.
- **Regression:** Predicting the selling price of a house based on its features.

## 16. What is Machine Learning? Explain types of Machine Learning.

**Machine Learning (ML):**
Machine learning is a subset of AI that enables computers to learn from data and make predictions or decisions without being explicitly programmed. It involves algorithms that improve automatically through experience by identifying patterns in data.

**Types of Machine Learning:**

1. **Supervised Learning:**
   - **Definition:** The model is trained on labeled data (input-output pairs) to learn a mapping from inputs to outputs.
   - **Objective:** Predict outputs for new, unseen data.
   - **Types:**
     - **Classification:** Predicts discrete class labels (e.g., spam detection).
     - **Regression:** Predicts continuous values (e.g., house prices).
   - **Algorithms:** Linear Regression, Logistic Regression, Decision Trees, SVM, Neural Networks.
   - **Applications:** Spam filtering, medical diagnosis, stock price prediction.

2. **Unsupervised Learning:**
   - **Definition:** The model is trained on unlabeled data to find hidden patterns or structures.
   - **Objective:** Discover inherent groupings or relationships in data.
   - **Types:**
     - **Clustering:** Groups similar data points (e.g., customer segmentation).
     - **Dimensionality Reduction:** Reduces the number of features while preserving structure (e.g., PCA).
   - **Algorithms:** K-Means, Hierarchical Clustering, DBSCAN, PCA, t-SNE.
   - **Applications:** Market basket analysis, anomaly detection, image compression.

3. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
   - **Definition:** The model learns by interacting with an environment, receiving rewards or penalties for actions.
   - **Objective:** Learn a policy that maximizes cumulative reward.
   - **Components:**
     - **Agent:** The learner or decision-maker.
     - **Environment:** The world the agent interacts with.
     - **Reward Signal:** Feedback from the environment.
   - **Algorithms:** Q-Learning, Deep Q Network (DQN), Policy Gradient Methods.
   - **Applications:** Game playing (AlphaGo), robotics, autonomous vehicles.

4. **Semi-Supervised Learning:**
   - **Definition:** Combines a small amount of labeled data with a large amount of unlabeled data.
   - **Objective:** Improve learning accuracy without requiring fully labeled data.
   - **Applications:** Speech recognition, web content classification.

5. **Self-Supervised Learning:**
   - **Definition:** A form of unsupervised learning where the model generates its own labels from the data.
   - **Objective:** Learn useful representations without human-labeled data.
   - **Applications:** Pre-training large language models (e.g., GPT).

## 17. Define Deep Learning, Compare biological Neural Network with Artificial Neural Network.

**Deep Learning:**
Deep learning is a subset of machine learning that uses artificial neural networks (ANNs) with many layers (hence "deep") to model complex patterns in data. It excels at tasks like image recognition, natural language processing, and speech recognition by automatically extracting hierarchical features from raw data.

**Comparison: Biological vs. Artificial Neural Networks**

| Feature          | Biological Neural Network (Human Brain)                                        | Artificial Neural Network (ANN)                                               |
|------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Basic Unit**   | Neuron                                                                         | Artificial Neuron (Node)                                                      |
| **Signal**       | Electrical impulses and chemical neurotransmitters.                            | Numeric values (weights and activations).                                     |
| **Learning**     | Synaptic plasticity (strengthening/weakening of connections based on activity). | Weight updates via backpropagation and optimization algorithms (e.g., SGD).   |
| **Speed**        | Slow (milliseconds per operation).                                             | Fast (billions of operations per second).                                     |
| **Energy Efficiency** | Highly energy-efficient (∼20 watts for the brain).                          | Energy-intensive (requires significant computational power).                  |
| **Adaptability** | Highly adaptable; can learn from minimal data and generalize well.             | Requires large datasets; struggles with generalization outside training data. |
| **Structure**    | Complex, dynamic, and three-dimensional.                                       | Simplified, layered, and often one-directional (in feedforward networks).    |
| **Fault Tolerance** | Robust; can function despite damage or noise.                                | Vulnerable to adversarial attacks and noisy data.                            |
| **Scalability**  | Limited by physical constraints (skull size).                                  | Highly scalable with computational resources.                                 |
| **Applications** | Natural intelligence, creativity, emotion.                                     | Image recognition, NLP, game playing, etc.                                   |

**Example:**
- **Biological:** A human recognizes a friend's face instantly, even in poor lighting.

## 18. Draw and Explain McCulloch-pitts Model.

**McCulloch-Pitts Neuron Model:**
Proposed in 1943 by McCulloch and Pitts, this model is the foundation of modern neural networks. It simulates the behavior of a biological neuron using a simplified computational model.

**Structure:**
1. **Inputs:** Binary values (0 or 1).
2. **Weights:** Binary weights (excitatory or inhibitory).
3. **Summing Function:** Computes the weighted sum of inputs.
4. **Activation Function:** A step function that outputs 1 if the sum ≥ threshold, else 0.

**Diagram:**
```
Inputs (x₁, x₂, ..., xₙ) → Weights (w₁, w₂, ..., wₙ) → Summing Function (Σ) → Activation Function (Step) → Output (y)
```

**Mathematical Representation:**
\[ y = \begin{cases} 
1 & \text{if } \sum_{i=1}^n w_i x_i \geq \text{threshold} \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases} \]

**Logic Gates Modeled:**
- **AND Gate:** Outputs 1 only if all inputs are 1.
- **OR Gate:** Outputs 1 if at least one input is 1.
- **NOT Gate:** Outputs the inverse of the input.

**Significance:**
- First computational model of a neuron.
- Demonstrated that simple neurons could perform logical operations.
- Paved the way for modern neural networks.

**Limitations:**
- Only binary inputs and outputs.
- No learning mechanism (weights are fixed).
- Cannot model complex or non-linear functions.

## 19. Explain Basic Types of Artificial Neural Network.

**Basic Types of Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs):**

1. **Feedforward Neural Networks (FNN):**
   - **Structure:** Data flows in one direction (input → hidden layers → output). No cycles or loops.
   - **Key Features:**
     - Fully connected layers.
     - Activation functions like ReLU, Sigmoid, Tanh.
   - **Applications:** Image classification, regression tasks.

2. **Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN):**
   - **Structure:** Cyclical connections allow feedback loops, enabling memory of previous inputs.
   - **Key Features:**
     - Handles sequential data (e.g., time series, text).
     - Variants: LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory), GRU (Gated Recurrent Unit) to address vanishing gradients.
   - **Applications:** Speech recognition, language modeling, time series prediction.

3. **Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN):**
   - **Structure:** Designed for grid-like data (e.g., images). Uses convolutional layers to extract features.
   - **Key Features:**
     - Local connectivity (reduces parameters).
     - Pooling layers for dimensionality reduction.
   - **Applications:** Image recognition, object detection, medical imaging.

4. **Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN):**
   - **Structure:** Consists of two networks—Generator (creates fake data) and Discriminator (evaluates authenticity).
   - **Key Features:**
     - Adversarial training improves both networks.
   - **Applications:** Image generation, deepfakes, art creation.

5. **Graph Neural Networks (GNN):**
   - **Structure:** Operates on graph data (nodes and edges).
   - **Key Features:**
     - Message passing between nodes to capture relationships.
   - **Applications:** Social network analysis, molecular chemistry, recommendation systems.

### 22. Write a Short note on Generative Adversarial Network.

**Generative Adversarial Network (GAN):**  
GAN is a deep learning framework introduced by Ian Goodfellow in 2014, where two neural networks—**Generator (G)** and **Discriminator (D)**—compete in a zero-sum game.  

**How GAN Works:**  
1. **Generator:**  
   - Creates fake data (e.g., images) from random noise.  
   - Goal: Fool the Discriminator into classifying fake data as real.  
2. **Discriminator:**  
   - Acts as a classifier, distinguishing real data from fake.  
   - Goal: Correctly identify fake data.  

**Training Process:**  
- **Adversarial Training:** G and D improve iteratively:  
  - G generates increasingly realistic data.  
  - D becomes better at detecting fakes.  
- Equilibrium: G produces data indistinguishable from real (D’s accuracy ≈ 50%).  

**Key Features:**  
- **Unsupervised Learning:** Requires no labeled data.  
- **Loss Functions:**  
  - Generator loss: Maximizes D’s errors on fake data.  
  - Discriminator loss: Maximizes accuracy on real vs. fake.  

**Applications:**  
- **Image Generation:** Creating realistic faces (e.g., StyleGAN).  
- **Art & Design:** AI-generated paintings, fashion designs.  
- **Data Augmentation:** Generating synthetic training data.  
- **Deepfakes:** Realistic video/audio manipulations (ethical concerns).  
- **Medical Imaging:** Generating synthetic scans for research.  

**Challenges:**  
- **Mode Collapse:** G produces limited varieties of outputs.  
- **Training Instability:** Balancing G and D is difficult.  
- **Ethical Risks:** Misuse for misinformation (e.g., fake news).  

**Example:**  
- **CycleGAN:** Converts horses to zebras (and vice versa) by learning unpaired image-to-image translation.

### 23. Write a short note on LLMs.

**Large Language Models (LLMs):**  
LLMs are AI models trained on vast text corpora to understand, generate, and manipulate human language. They leverage **transformer architectures** (e.g., GPT, BERT) for context-aware processing.  

**Key Features:**  
1. **Scale:**  
   - Billions of parameters (e.g., GPT-3: 175B parameters).  
   - Trained on diverse datasets (books, articles, code).  
2. **Architecture:**  
   - **Self-Attention:** Weights word importance dynamically (e.g., "bank" in "river bank" vs. "bank account").  
   - **Positional Encoding:** Captures word order without RNNs.  
3. **Capabilities:**  
   - Text completion, translation, summarization, Q&A, code generation.  
   - Few-shot learning (performs tasks with minimal examples).  

**Applications:**  
- **Chatbots:** ChatGPT, Gemini for conversational AI.  
- **Content Creation:** Writing articles, marketing copy.  
- **Education:** Tutoring, explaining concepts.  
- **Research:** Summarizing papers, extracting insights.  

**Challenges:**  
- **Bias:** Reflects prejudices in training data.  
- **Hallucinations:** Generates plausible but false information.  
- **Energy Consumption:** High computational cost (e.g., GPT-3’s training ≈ 1,300 MWh).  

**Example Models:**  
- **GPT-4:** General-purpose text generation.  
- **BLOOM:** Multilingual LLM supporting 46 languages.  
- **Krutrim:** India’s LLM for regional languages.

### 24. Explain How ChatGPT works & List Limitations of ChatGPT.

**How ChatGPT Works:**  
1. **Architecture:**  
   - Based on **GPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer)**, a decoder-only transformer.  
   - Uses **self-attention** to weigh word relevance in context.  
2. **Training:**  
   - **Pre-training:** Learns language patterns from diverse text (books, websites).  
   - **Fine-tuning:** Refines on human-annotated data for safety/alignment.  
3. **Inference:**  
   - Predicts next word iteratively (autoregressive generation).  
   - Uses **temperature** and **top-k sampling** to balance creativity/coherence.  

**Limitations of ChatGPT:**  
1. **Factual Errors:**  
   - Generates plausible but incorrect answers ("hallucinations").  
2. **Bias:**  
   - Reflects biases in training data (e.g., gender stereotypes).  
3. **Context Window:**  
   - Limited memory (~8K tokens in GPT-4); loses track in long conversations.  
4. **No Real-Time Knowledge:**  
   - Training data cutoff (e.g., GPT-4’s knowledge ends in 2023).  
5. **Verbosity:**  
   - Over-explains or avoids direct answers.  
6. **Security Risks:**  
   - Potential for misuse (phishing, misinformation).  
7. **Computational Cost:**  
   - High energy use for inference/training.  

**Example:**  
- If asked, "Who won the 2024 Olympics?" (post-cutoff), ChatGPT may fabricate an answer.
